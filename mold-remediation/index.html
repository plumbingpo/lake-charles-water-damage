Mold Remediation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly mold issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Laundry rooms, baths and kitchens all need working exhaust ventilation that is sized for the space
  • Mold in ductwork is prevented by servicing the heating and cooling system and replacing its filters on schedule
  • Use ventilation and a dehumidifier to hold humidity under 60%, with 30 to 50% as the ideal
  • Clear gutters and downspouts regularly, which keeps rain from soaking the foundation walls
  • When windows, piping or other cold surfaces start to sweat, correct it without waiting
  • Do not leave damp laundry, bath mats or spilled water sitting for long

Why Professional Mold Remediation Matters

Removing mold is work homeowners should not attempt on their own. Without containment, scrubbing or tearing out moldy material releases spores by the millions, potentially spreading contamination through the house and making the health exposure worse than it was. Our crews work in personal protective equipment inside a negative-pressure enclosure, with HEPA air scrubbers running, to trap spores and hold them in the work area while removal is underway.

A professional job goes after the moisture source too, because that is why the mold grew in the first place. Clean off the visible growth but leave the moisture problem, and the mold comes back, in many cases in a matter of weeks. Finding concealed moisture, drying it properly and preventing a repeat all take equipment and training, which certified remediation technicians have.

How to Tell You Need Mold Remediation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Recurring respiratory symptoms, allergies or headaches that improve when you are away from home
  • Recent water damage or flooding that was not professionally dried
  • Condensation forming regularly on windows, walls or cold surfaces
  • Cleaning that never lasts, with growth returning to the same surface
  • A slow leak under a sink, behind an appliance or in the roof that went unnoticed
  • Dark staining or discoloration on walls, ceilings or around HVAC vents
  • Fuzzy or spotted growth on stored boxes, clothing or the backs of furniture
