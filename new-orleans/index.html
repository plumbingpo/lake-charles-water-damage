Common Water Damage Issues in New Orleans

Some water problems need a phone call this minute, and others can safely wait until morning. We have ordered these five common New Orleans calls by how fast each one needs attention.

Bursts: A pipe that has split is the most urgent call there is, because it puts out water for as long as the supply is on. Depending on the season, the break may follow a freeze, and it often shows only when the ice inside melts. Close the main valve, open a faucet to relieve pressure and call right away, even at night. Ceilings that bulge with trapped water should be left alone and the room below kept empty.

Runoff: Storm water coming under a door or through a garage is next in line. There is no valve to close here, so lift what you can off the floor and avoid wet electrical outlets. Having passed over pavement and soil on the way in, it carries dirt and germs. The Lake Charles Restoration team extracts it once conditions are safe, then removes soaked materials that cannot be cleaned.

Kitchens: Leaks from a dishwasher, an ice maker line or the plumbing under the kitchen sink should be dealt with the day you find them. Close the small valve under the sink or behind the appliance and the flow usually stops. What remains is the water trapped below the cabinets and floor covering, and it will not dry by itself. In New Orleans kitchens we often dry cabinets in place by sending air through small holes in the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Dampness in the space below the floor can wait a few days, though not a few months. When we look underneath, we tend to find water lying on the liner, insulation that is soaked or sweating ducts. Given time, that humidity works its way upstairs and the joists start to rot. Our crew clears any standing water, brings the framing down to a normal moisture level and reports the likely cause.

Odors: Last on the list is a stale, earthy odor in a room that once had a leak, which is worth a scheduled visit. Mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a material getting wet, so a leak from months ago had plenty of time. Do not scrub or spray a large patch yourself, because disturbing it spreads spores through the house. We check for hidden dampness, set up containment and clear out the affected material for homeowners in New Orleans.

Found Water in Your New Orleans Home? Start Here

What you should do before we arrive depends on what kind of water you are looking at. Clean water from a supply pipe or a tap left running is the least risky. You can walk in it with shoes on, soak it up, and move belongings out of it. Water from a washing machine, dishwasher or aquarium is dirtier, so wear gloves and wash your hands afterward. Anything from a sewer, or water that came in from outside, is hazardous, so shut the room and stay away from it.

Whatever the type, a few things help our New Orleans crew start faster. Clear a path from the door to the wet area and move cars so the truck can park close. Note the time you discovered the problem and your best guess at the source. Find your insurance policy number if it is easy to get to.

Time matters because clean water does not stay clean. Left sitting in carpet and drywall, it picks up contamination and can support mold within 24 to 48 hours. That is why the opening hour counts, even when a New Orleans leak seems small.

Simple Ways to Keep a New Orleans Home Dry

A home usually gives a few hints before a leak turns serious. Our technicians see the same early clues again and again in New Orleans, and they are worth learning. Paint that bubbles or peels, a brown ring on a ceiling, baseboards pulling away from the wall, and flooring that cups or lifts all mean moisture behind the surface. A musty smell in a closet, a toilet that rocks, or a tap that drips belongs on the list too.

Taken singly they are minor, though every one of them gets more expensive with time. Re-caulk tubs and showers when the bead cracks, since water behind tile does damage you will not see for months. If you have a sump pump, pour a bucket of water into the pit a few times a year to confirm it switches on. In basements and garages, shelving and plastic tubs keep belongings clear of the floor.

Good upkeep lowers the odds, but it cannot remove them. What it does is make them rarer and smaller, and that matters when the alternative is days of drying equipment in your New Orleans living room.
